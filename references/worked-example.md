# Worked Example: Compare, Preserve, Repair

This is an illustrative self-audit, not a record of an independent review or evidence that the proposed product works.

## Prompt

Explore divergent GTM frames for a reasoning-audit website that checks strategic writing for cognitive bias. Avoid generic SEO, ads, outbound, PLG, community, partnerships, and launch posts.

## Bubbles and attractors

Ban channel lists, generic free trials, community-building, and vague partnerships. Likely attractors include audit badges, compliance gates, public scoreboards, and training curricula. Source worlds such as customs, legal defense, private rehearsal, and apprenticeship are useful only when they change the adoption mechanism; renaming a badge is insufficient.

## First frames

| ID | Frame | Mechanism and relevant dependency |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Upstream certification mandate | A decision receiver rejects unaudited memos, forcing writers to attach audit proof before approval. Depends on the receiver having authority and wanting to enforce it. |
| F2 | Vendor reasoning attestation | Procurement rejects vendor proposals without an audited reasoning appendix. Adoption is required to pass an acceptance gate. |
| F3 | Diligence defense trail | Teams voluntarily keep a record of what they checked because they expect it to help explain a consequential decision later. Depends on perceived defensive value, not a submission requirement; no legal protection is assumed. |
| F4 | Reader counterargument | A reader voluntarily seeks an independent challenge to a memo before deciding what to believe. The audit supplies a counterargument for the reader's own judgment; nobody requires the author to attach proof. |
| F5 | Doubt itch loop | A writer's private uncertainty triggers a scan for relief or a useful catch. The loop fails if findings feel noisy or shaming. |
| F6 | Bias-card curriculum | An instructor uses repeated exercises to imprint a shared decision-quality vocabulary into a cohort. It fails if the vocabulary never enters later practice. |

## Collision check

Review status: `provisional: self-audit only`.
Spread verdict: `usable`; F1/F2 repeat one engine and need a merge or repair.

| Closest pair / family | IDs | Relationship and reason | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acceptance gate | F1, F2 | Duplicate at this level: both make a submitter attach audit evidence to gain acceptance from a receiver. Changing execs to procurement does not alter that dependency. | Keep F1; replace F2. |
| Audit evidence | F1, F3 | Related but distinct: remove the receiver's power to reject, and F1 fails; F3 can still motivate a voluntary record for later explanation. Shared proof vocabulary is insufficient to merge them. | Retain both; do not infer actual legal protection. |
| Trust and private doubt | F1, F4, F5 | F4 does not require submission or authority. F4 and F5 are closer: a reader challenges someone else's claim to decide belief, while a writer checks their own reasoning for relief/correction. That role in the judgment process matters here, though both could sit in a broader private-uncertainty family. | Retain the stated distinction; do not claim unrelatedness. |
| Habit formation | F5, F6 | A private uncertainty cue differs from repeated instruction that establishes a learned practice. | Retain. |

## Repair

Replace F2 with **Procurement objection mirror**: a vendor uses a buyer's specific objection to identify the unsupported reasoning in its proposal, reducing ambiguity in the next discussion. This depends on the buyer sharing a concrete objection.

The decisive change is that a voluntarily supplied objection steers revision; proof is no longer the price of passing a mandatory gate. Merely changing actor, trigger, and carrier would not establish that difference.

Recheck F2 against F1 and F4: F1 forces compliance; F4 helps a reader decide belief; repaired F2 helps a writer respond to an expressed objection. These remain related uses of an audit, with different causal dependencies. The revised set is still `provisional: self-audit only`.

## Optional landing

If asked to narrow, F1 explores receiver authority, F5 explores a private habit, and F6 explores learning through cohorts. Choose across those mechanisms according to the user's objective. Any claim that an institution would enforce an audit gate remains an assumption until tested.

## When not to force a repair

If F4 had only said “a trust handshake,” it would be unresolved. Ask what it actually changes or keep it as a fragment for development. Do not invent the voluntary-reading mechanism to certify it as distinct, and do not discard it solely because a checker grouped the word “trust.”
